## 1. NIH Parent SBIR PA-27-100

- Funding: Phase I caps vary by institute. NHLBI and NHGRI allow up to $400,000; NINDS
  allows up to $700,000; NIAMS and NLM use the current SBA guideline.
- Immediate deadline: the standard 2026-09-05 date moves to Tuesday, 2026-09-08 at
  5:00 p.m. local time because of the weekend and federal holiday.
- Next cycle: 2027-01-05, then 2027-04-05.
- Entity: a qualifying US small business. The PI must be primarily employed by the company,
  the company must perform at least 67 percent of the Phase I research effort, and SAM,
  UEI, Grants.gov, eRA Commons, and SBA Company Registry registrations must be active.
- Fit: use EDS as the rare-disease anchor and POTS as a major comorbidity and discovery
  problem. The funded object must be a commercializable technical product with a risky
  R&D question, such as validated cross-disease evidence retrieval, mechanistic hypothesis
  ranking, or research-cohort matching. Continued database curation alone will not clear
  the SBIR bar.
- Likely institute homes: NLM for biomedical informatics, NIAMS for connective tissue,
  NHLBI or NINDS for autonomic and cardiovascular mechanisms. Contact program staff before
  choosing the institute.
- Decision gate: September 8 is viable only if all registrations are already active and
  most of the scientific application exists. Otherwise use January 5.
- Source: https://files.simpler.grants.gov/opportunities/d1ba49e5-3684-4420-849a-ab2330ec493e/attachments/743ad4bd-fc28-450e-9249-1008f37490af/PA-27-100-Revised-Full-Announcement.html

## 2. NLnet Restack

- Funding: EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 for a first project, with larger follow-on support possible.
- Opens: 2026-09-03.
- Deadline: 2026-11-03 at 12:00 CEST.
- Entity: individuals, companies, and other organizations can receive support. A US proposal
  needs a clear European dimension and exceptional technical relevance.
- Fit: fund a technical Open POTS layer, such as an offline-first and privacy-preserving
  evidence browser, reproducible packaging, open standards, accessibility, or research tooling.
  Do not pitch it as a medical information website.
- Source: https://nlnet.nl/restack/ and https://nlnet.nl/propose/

## 3. Sentient Foundation Open Source AGI Grants

- Funding: amount per award is not published. The total committed pool is $42 million.
- Cycle: rolling, no deadline.
- Entity: open-source maintainers, researchers, and public-goods builders. The grant track
  states no equity, lockup, or claim on the work.
- Fit: propose a local, private, open-weight POTS evidence assistant that works from the
  existing public dataset and repository. The current database alone is not enough of an
  open-AI proposal.
- Source: https://sentient.foundation/grants

## 4. National Library of Medicine PAR-26-040

- Funding: up to $250,000 per year in direct costs for up to four years.
- Next deadline: 2026-10-05 at 5:00 p.m. local time.
- Entity: US small businesses and other for-profit organizations are eligible. NIH, SAM,
  Grants.gov, and eRA Commons registrations are required.
- Fit: potentially excellent for a new, broadly reusable bioinformatics method, pipeline,
  or resource built and validated through POTS. A POTS-only database or maintenance proposal
  is explicitly too narrow. The method must generalize beyond one disease and represent a
  significant technical advance.
- Decision gate: send the scientific contact a one-page fit inquiry before drafting an R01.
- Source: https://files.simpler.grants.gov/opportunities/ca108e77-69f6-4d3a-9723-a1353076a4ad/attachments/1882a2af-b08d-49bf-8edd-08ac7ad5facd/PAR-26-040-Full-Announcement.html

## New, but not worth submitting now

- Open Science Foundation: project grants and fellowships are expected to open in Q4 2026,
  but award size and eligibility are not published. Watch: https://opening.science/
- ARO Foundation: open and fast, but its actual focus is blockchain and digital-economy
  infrastructure. Open POTS would be a forced fit.
- Open Source Endowment: grants top out at $5,000 and exclude projects associated with a
  startup, which likely blocks the LLC route.
- CSL Patient Organisation Grants: EDS provides a plausible rare-disease anchor, but direct
  LLC eligibility is unclear and the program funds patient-organization programming rather
  than the core R&D product.
- SHRARE Rare Assembly Grant: service credits rather than cash, with commercial and IP terms
  that conflict with the current funding screen.

## NIH SBIR readiness checklist

Empowered Methods may be company-eligible if it is a US for-profit LLC, is primarily US
operated, is more than 50 percent owned and controlled by US citizens or permanent residents,
and has no more than 500 employees including affiliates. The application still needs:

- An eligible PI whose primary employment is with the small business during the project.
- At least 67 percent of Phase I research or analytical work performed by the small business.
- A commercializable product or service with a risky technical feasibility question. A public
  dataset or routine curation project by itself is not enough.
- Active SAM.gov registration, UEI and CAGE information, SBA Company Registry registration,
  Grants.gov registration, and eRA Commons registration for the organization and PI.
- An eRA Signing Official and PI account, plus a linked ORCID for every PI.

NIH says registrations can take six weeks or more. If Erin cannot verify every registration
and a company-employed PI, September 8 is not a real submission date. January 5 is the
appropriate target. STTR is the alternative when the project depends on a formal nonprofit
research-institution partner and the PI arrangement is better there.
