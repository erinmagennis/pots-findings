SCOPE — a biomarker/mechanism corpus for POTS and RELATED conditions. The research programme is
"biological markers and causes of POTS and related conditions": dysautonomia BROADLY, fibromyalgia,
Ehlers-Danlos/hypermobility, ME/CFS, IBS, migraine, autism, mast cell disease, small-fibre neuropathy.

IN SCOPE is BROAD and the lists below are ILLUSTRATIVE, NOT EXHAUSTIVE. In particular:
* ANY dysautonomia or autonomic-nervous-system disorder is IN SCOPE — including familial dysautonomia
  (Riley-Day, ELP1/HSAN III), hereditary sensory and autonomic neuropathies, multiple system atrophy,
  pure autonomic failure, orthostatic hypotension, orthostatic intolerance, syncope and convulsive
  syncope, vasovagal syncope, baroreflex failure, autonomic testing/QSART studies.
* ANY headache/cephalalgia disorder is in scope for the migraine layer — including trigeminal autonomic
  cephalalgias, cluster headache, SUNCT/SUNA, hemicrania, new daily persistent headache, and secondary
  headache when autonomic features are studied.
* Connective-tissue and hypermobility disorders: Ehlers-Danlos (all types), hypermobility spectrum
  disorder, joint hypermobility, Marfan, Loeys-Dietz, osteogenesis imperfecta.
* Mast cell: MCAS, systemic mastocytosis, hereditary alpha-tryptasemia.
* Post-infectious/post-viral: long COVID, PASC, post-viral fatigue, ME/CFS, PANS/PANDAS.
* Sjogren syndrome, other autoimmune rheumatic disease WHEN studied for neurological, autonomic,
  fatigue, small-fibre, or biomarker aspects.
* Comorbidity, epidemiology, mechanism, biomarker, genetics, treatment, and patient-experience studies
  of any of the above ALL COUNT as in scope.

OFF TOPIC means one of two specific things:
(1) WRONG ABBREVIATION SENSE — the paper uses an abbreviation in a sense foreign to this corpus:
    HSD = hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase or honey-sweetened drink (not hypermobility spectrum disorder);
    ASD = atrial septal defect or adult spinal deformity (not autism spectrum disorder);
    EDS = excessive daytime sleepiness or electronic data systems (not Ehlers-Danlos);
    SS = systemic sclerosis or steady-state (not Sjogren) — BUT systemic sclerosis studied for
      autonomic/small-fibre involvement is in scope;
    PANS/PANDAS = polyarteritis nodosa or the animal (not the paediatric neuropsychiatric syndrome);
    MS = mass spectrometry used as a mere method label; MD = "mean difference"; CM = Chiari malformation.
(2) TOPICAL DRIFT — an orthopaedic/sports-medicine joint-laxity paper (clubfoot Ponseti, hallux valgus,
    ACL injury, TMJ prolotherapy) or a rare multi-system genetic syndrome where "joint hypermobility"
    is one incidental phenotype item in a long list, with no dysautonomia/connective-tissue/biomarker
    research question.

When a paper studies one of many diseases including an in-scope one (e.g. a survey across rheumatic
diseases that includes Sjogren), that is IN SCOPE — cross-mention data is used by this corpus.
If genuinely torn, answer "uncertain". Do NOT answer off_topic merely because a condition is not
named in the illustrative lists above.
